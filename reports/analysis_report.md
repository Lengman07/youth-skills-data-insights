# Youth Skills Program — Data & Insights Report

## 1. Executive Summary

The analysis evaluated 15 participants across three training programs:
Data Analytics, Web Development, and Cybersecurity.

Overall, participants recorded an average score improvement of 23.73
points, with an average attendance rate of 83.2%. The overall employment
rate was 66.67%, while average participant satisfaction was 4.13 out of 5.

A strong positive association was observed between attendance and learning
improvement (r = 0.894). Participants who were employed also had higher
average attendance and learning improvement than unemployed participants.

---

## 2. Key Findings

### Training Performance

Data Analytics recorded the highest average score improvement at 25.0
points and the highest average satisfaction at 4.33 out of 5.

Cybersecurity recorded the highest observed employment rate at 75%.

Web Development recorded an average score improvement of 23.6 points and
an employment rate of 60%.

### Attendance and Learning Outcomes

Average attendance across all participants was 83.2%.

The correlation between attendance and score improvement was approximately
0.894, indicating a strong positive association in this sample.

Employed participants had an average attendance of 90.1%, compared with
69.4% among unemployed participants.

### Regional Performance

Eastern recorded the highest average score improvement at 29.67 points,
while Ashanti recorded the lowest at 19.67 points.

Regional comparisons should be interpreted cautiously because the number
of participants per region was small.

### Education and Employment

Participants with tertiary education had an observed employment rate of
90%, compared with 20% among participants with SHS education.

This is a substantial difference, but the small sample size means the
result should not be interpreted as evidence that education level alone
causes employment outcomes.

### Participants Requiring Attention

Three participants, representing 20% of the sample, were classified as
"Needs Attention" based on the project criteria of:

- Attendance below 75%
- Score improvement below 15 points

All three participants were unemployed.

---

## 3. Recommendations

### 1. Strengthen Attendance Monitoring

Introduce early attendance monitoring and follow-up for participants
whose attendance begins to fall below expected levels.

### 2. Provide Targeted Support

Participants with both low attendance and low learning improvement should
receive additional mentoring, academic support, or engagement interventions.

### 3. Investigate Employment Pathways

Further investigate why employment outcomes differ across programs,
particularly the stronger observed employment performance of Cybersecurity.

### 4. Support Participants With Lower Educational Backgrounds

Consider additional foundational support for participants entering the
program with SHS-level education, particularly around employability and
technical readiness.

### 5. Continue Monitoring Program Outcomes

Track attendance, assessment improvement, satisfaction, and employment
outcomes over multiple cohorts to determine whether the patterns observed
in this sample remain consistent.

---

## 4. Limitations

The analysis is based on only 15 participants.

The small sample size limits the ability to generalize the findings to the
entire youth training population.

The analysis identifies associations rather than causal relationships.

Regional and demographic comparisons should therefore be interpreted
carefully and validated using larger datasets.
